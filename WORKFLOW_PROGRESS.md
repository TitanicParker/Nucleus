## How to use this file

This document records completed workflow acts in sequence.

Each act should capture:

- what body was engaged;
- when and how it was engaged;
- what was submitted;
- what part of the controlling suspicion the act was designed to test or affect;
- what the body was asked to decide or provide;
- what the body was **not** being asked to decide;
- what evidential or institutional state existed before the act;
- what state transition the act created;
- what response, acknowledgement or outcome followed;
- what remains unanswered;

The governing repository principle is:

> **One mechanism, mandate-specific examinations.**

An outbound submission is not treated as an endpoint. It becomes part of the repository's evidential and institutional state and must be available to the next workflow agent before another body is selected.

## Status

## Subject

**Article 15 GDPR Access Request — Provenance of July 2017 Clinical Synthesis and 2025 PAR/SIMT Review**

## Procedural context

Before submitting the Article 15 request, the Data Protection Commission was contacted on 31 August 2026 in relation to existing complaint reference **C-25-7-2074**.

The DPC was informed that a further, narrowly targeted Article 15 request was being considered concerning two distinct information objects.

The DPC subsequently confirmed that it was the data subject's right to raise further requests with HSE / University Hospital Galway, while also confirming that the existing DPC complaint would remain confined to the matters already identified within that complaint.

The Article 15 request was therefore submitted as a **new and standalone controller request**, rather than as an extension of the existing DPC complaint.

---

## Purpose of Act 1

This request does not primarily seek another copy of already-known final documents.

It seeks the personal data, metadata, audit information, source information and processing history capable of revealing **how two authoritative information objects were actually produced**:

1. the July 2017 Neurology discharge / clinical synthesis; and
2. the 2025 Preliminary Assessment Report / Serious Incident Management Team review.

The request therefore operates on two different stages of the suspected mechanism:

- **Object A tests construction of the founding information state.**
- **Object B tests later institutional validation of that information state.**

# Object A — July 2017 Neurology discharge / clinical synthesis

## Object

The detailed Neurology clinical/discharge narrative arising from:

- admission beginning 3 July 2017;
- physical discharge on 11 July 2017;
- later DaT scan / addendum material;
- transcription finalisation;
- later consultant confirmation/finalisation.

The request treats the final document as a temporally layered information object whose production history is distinct from the surviving final text.

## Core workflow purpose

**Construction / provenance test**

Object A is intended to determine, insofar as the retained systems permit:

> **How was the founding July 2017 information state sourced, authored, dictated, transcribed, revised, supplemented, confirmed, finalised and distributed?**

This directly tests the construction stage of the controlling suspicion.

It is especially relevant to the question whether the final specialist synthesis can be reconstructed as a sequence of information-processing events rather than treated as a single undifferentiated historical document.

The request sought available source information for personal data incorporated into the final synthesis, including where applicable:

### A7 — 13 July addendum and 17 July confirmation

### A8 — Distribution and recipients

The request sought:

Object A is designed to test the **construction stage** of the suspected mechanism.

Relevant questions include:

- When did the disputed formulations first exist?
- What source material preceded them?
- Were they dictated initially or introduced later?
- Were substantive changes made after physical discharge?
- Was the main narrative still editable after the alleged Protest?

The repository contained a reconstructed final clinical narrative and a controlling suspicion concerning its function, but did not possess the native production history required to establish exactly how that information object was generated.

### State transition created by Act 1 / Object A

GUH was formally placed on notice that the patient seeks the **native provenance of the founding 2017 information object**, including its source genealogy, versions, audit history and lifecycle.

This converts provenance from an analytical question in the repository into a live controller request capable of producing independent technical evidence.

Object B tests whether the 2025 governance process constituted an **independent reconstruction** or whether it may have treated inherited documentary descendants as validation of the founding information state.

Relevant questions include:

- What primary evidence was reviewed?
- Did reviewers reconstruct the 2017 evidence independently?
- Did they distinguish copied/inherited propositions from genuinely new evidence?
- Was an independent clinical expert used?
- What sources supported the eventual conclusions?

- Did any Article 18 restriction, warning or annotation actually accompany the information under review?
- What version of the evidence was presented to SIMT?
- Can the `"no deficit in care"` conclusion be traced through its evidential genealogy?

**Article 16 GDPR — Request to Complete July 2017 Neurology Record by Supplementary Statement**

## Attachment

**Patient Supplementary Statement — July 2017 Neurology Discharge / Clinical Synthesis**

---

## Purpose of Act 2

The Article 16 request does **not** seek:

- deletion of the 2017 record;
- alteration of the historical clinician-authored text;
- retrospective rewriting of diagnosis;
- retrospective rewriting of clinical reasoning.

It asks that the original historical document remain intact.

The requested action is to have the patient's supplementary statement:

> **durably attached to, or directly cross-referenced with, the July 2017 Neurology discharge / clinical synthesis so that its existence is apparent whenever that synthesis is subsequently accessed, reviewed, relied upon, disclosed or otherwise processed.**

If direct annotation is technically unavailable, GUH was asked to identify the mechanism by which the statement would remain meaningfully and durably associated with the historical synthesis.

---

# Content of the supplementary statement

The statement is explicitly presented as the patient's own recollection.

It records the following account:

- On **Monday 10 July 2017**, after being informed that discharge would occur the following day, the patient became concerned that the painful foot condition forming an important part of the referral had not, in his view, been meaningfully investigated beyond the initial examination.
- Four medical students were present at the bedside.
- The patient asked them to get the treating neurologist because discharge was planned and he needed to speak about his feet.
- The neurologist attended.
- The curtain was drawn.
- The patient recalls saying words to the effect:

> “I’m being discharged tomorrow. Do you mean to tell me that you’re going to bring me in here and give me the full NCT and send me out on bald tyres?”

- The patient recalls the response:

> “It’s as easy for you to get a podiatry appointment as me.”

- The exchange then ended.
- The patient was discharged the following day.
- The patient states that he does not recall a further focused neurological examination or reassessment of the relationship between the painful feet and neurological lower-limb abnormalities after that interaction and before discharge.

The statement explains that the surviving July 2017 synthesis contains extensive personal data about:

- the patient's questions;
- understanding of diagnosis and treatment strategy;
- discussions/debriefing;
- the existence of a `"clear plan"`;
- disposition of the foot problem;

but does not contain this alleged pre-discharge challenge.

The supplementary statement therefore asks that the record also preserve the patient's account that, before discharge, he expressly challenged the adequacy of the investigation/disposition of the presenting foot problem.

---

## Evidential status

The supplementary statement does **not** establish as documentary fact that the 10 July 2017 interaction occurred exactly as recalled.

Its case-specific evidential status is:

`patient evidence`

What is independently established by the 2 September 2026 submission is that:

- the patient formally placed this account before GUH;
- identified it as his recollection;
- requested preservation of the account alongside the challenged information object;
- expressly avoided requesting alteration or destruction of the historical clinician record.

This distinction must be preserved in future repository processing.

---

## Relationship to the controlling suspicion

Act 2 addresses the **continuing-propagation stage** of the suspected mechanism.

The controlling suspicion treats the alleged Protest as an epistemic warning that would materially alter how later readers interpret the apparent completeness/closure of the 2017 specialist synthesis.

If GUH gives effect to the Article 16 request, future processing of the 2017 synthesis should no longer occur without an accessible indication that a contrary patient account exists.

Act 2 therefore attempts to intervene not in the historical construction of the object, but in its **future informational behaviour**.

The sequence is:

1. **Construction** — investigated by Article 15 Object A.
2. **Retrospective institutional validation** — investigated by Article 15 Object B.
3. **Continuing propagation** — acted upon by Article 16.

---

## Protection of Article 15 provenance

The Article 16 request expressly states that it is separate from the Article 15 request of 31 August 2026.

It also expressly states that nothing in the Article 16 request is intended to overwrite, alter or remove:

- historical versions;
- metadata;
- audit information;
- other material potentially responsive to Article 15.

This is important because Act 2 is designed to **supplement the future information state without destroying evidence about the historical information state.**

---
